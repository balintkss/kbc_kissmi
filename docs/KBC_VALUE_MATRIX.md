# KBC Digital Twin: Product, Market and Value Matrix

Presentation positioning: **KBC Digital Twin is the customer-governed financial memory for Kate.**

It is not another chatbot, a generic next-best-offer engine, or a claim that KBC sees a customer's whole life. Kate remains the interface. The Twin is the explainable decision layer used by Kate, KBC Mobile, kbc.be and advisers.

## 1. The strategic answer

KBC already has a broad retail offer, Kate, digital distribution, proactive services and a bank-insurance relationship. The credible gap is not more data collection. It is one shared, customer-visible context that answers four questions before KBC acts:

1. **What do we believe, and what evidence supports it?**
2. **Is that belief useful and permitted for this purpose?**
3. **Does the customer confirm it and want KBC to remember it?**
4. **Does the next step advance the customer's stated goal, rather than merely create a sale?**

Call this the **Financial Understanding Contract**:

```text
Observed, non-sensitive financial pattern
        -> explainable hypothesis (confidence + expiry)
        -> customer confirms, edits, rejects or ignores
        -> customer-governed Financial Memory
        -> goal and affordability check
        -> one helpful next step, with explicit approval for any action
```

The winning line:

> **KBC already has the products and Kate already has the conversation. The Digital Twin gives both a customer-owned memory: the context to know when to help, the discipline to know when not to sell, and the proof to explain every recommendation.**

## 3. Why KBC can win — without overclaiming

### The honest competitive position

Every serious Belgian bank has payment data, a mobile channel, lending/saving products and some form of digital assistance. Consent-based aggregators and general AI assistants can also obtain partial account data. Therefore do **not** say that competitors or big-tech assistants have no data.

KBC's defensible advantage is the combination of:

1. **Permissioned first-party financial context** when KBC is the customer's primary relationship;
2. **Bank-insurance breadth** across accounts, saving, lending, insurance and advice;
3. **Kate and KBC Mobile as an existing trusted interaction and execution channel**; and
4. **A human route** when a choice becomes regulated, consequential or emotionally sensitive.

KBC reported that 77% of active Belgian clients had a bank-insurance relationship at year-end 2024; more than nine in ten KBC mortgage clients also bought KBC home insurance. Those are KBC-reported figures, not market-wide facts. [KBC 2024 annual report](https://www.kbc.com/content/dam/kbccom/doc/investor-relations/Results/jvs-2024/jvs-2024-gr-en.pdf?zone=footer-2)

The trend validates the category, but does not remove the need for trust. Meta launched Muse as a proactive personal AI agent, while OpenAI's US-only ChatGPT Finances connects user-selected accounts through Plaid. Generic assistants may understand a conversation; KBC can, with permission, connect a financial insight to an existing banking, insurance or adviser journey. [Meta Muse](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/09/introducing-muse-personal-ai-agent/amp/) · [OpenAI Finances](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001222-finances-in-chatgpt)

Use this slide wording:

> **Big-tech agents may understand a conversation. KBC can, with permission, understand the financial reality behind it — and help safely turn insight into action.**

## 5. Trust is the product, not a disclaimer

The user's long-term relationship with KBC is earned by what the system refuses to infer or sell.

### Required product rules

1. **Hypothesis, not hidden fact.** A transaction pattern creates a low-confidence suggestion, never a secret permanent profile.
2. **Confirmation before memory.** The customer can confirm, edit, reject, ignore or delete it. Only confirmed facts can drive a proactive commercial journey.
3. **Visible purpose.** Every card exposes *why KBC is asking*, evidence, confidence, expiry and what will change if the customer confirms.
4. **Sensitive-data firewall.** Never infer or retain health, mental health, pregnancy, religion, politics, sexual orientation, ethnicity or trade-union membership from transactions, merchant names or chat.
5. **Support before sales.** Financial stress suppresses sales messages; the system offers a plan, options and human help instead.
6. **No autonomous high-stakes decision.** The Twin can prepare a journey; lending, insurance pricing/underwriting, investments and money movement remain in existing regulated flows with human or explicit-customer approval.
7. **External data stays optional.** If KBC later uses external accounts, access must be explicit and revocable under PSD2.

The EDPB warns that combinations of payment data can reveal special-category information and recommends precise data mapping and impact assessment. [EDPB PSD2-GDPR guidance](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sites/default/files/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_202006_psd2_afterpublicconsultation_en.pdf) The GDPR also protects people from solely automated decisions with legal or similarly significant effects. [GDPR Article 22](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2016/679/oj)

### Replace risky examples in every pitch and UI

| Do not say | Say instead |
|---|---|
| "We see you visit a psychologist." | "We found a recurring payment. Would you like it included in your financial plan?" |
| "You have a Whoop, so you are health-conscious." | "Would you like this recurring subscription treated as fitness, mobility, learning, other, or excluded?" |
| "You bought pet food, so you have a dog." | "We noticed recurring pet-related spending. Would you like to add a pet to your financial plan?" |
| "We know everything about you." | "We build a partial, customer-controlled picture from the financial data you allow us to use." |

## 6. The monthly check-in that does not feel like surveillance

Do not interrogate customers after every merchant transaction. Run a quiet, customer-controlled **Financial Understanding Check-in** at most monthly, or only when the customer opens it.

It asks at most one or two high-value, non-sensitive questions:

- "Your plan has recurring mobility costs. Should we plan for a car, shared mobility, or neither?"
- "Would you like this recurring subscription to appear in your monthly plan?"
- "Your essential bills are close to next month's income. Would you like to protect your savings goal and see non-sales options?"

Each question contains **Why am I seeing this?**, **Not now**, and **Don't use this category**. The customer is improving their own plan, not completing a hidden marketing profile.

## 7. The 45-second judge answer

> KBC does not need another chatbot. Kate already has the conversation, and KBC already has products across banking and insurance. Our Digital Twin is the customer-governed financial memory underneath them. It turns a partial financial trail into an evidence-backed hypothesis, asks the customer to confirm it, then uses only that consented context to protect their goal and suggest one useful next step. Every channel sees the same context, every recommendation is explainable, and when money is tight, the system stops selling. That is why it improves KBC's entire retail proposition rather than adding one more feature.
